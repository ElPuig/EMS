# Applying for an Erasmus+ internship

Apply for an Erasmus+ internship abroad and follow your application from the student portal.

**Who:** students of a vocational training cycle (CFGM or CFGS), any year, while a call is open.

**Your emails:** Erasmus+ emails reach you at your school address (the Puig one), not your personal one. The only exception is the email to sign a document electronically, which you can also sign from the portal. When your family gets a copy, it gets it at its own address.

---

## 1. Open the Erasmus+ page

In the portal, click the **Erasmus+** card, or **Erasmus+** in the top menu. Both only appear when there is a call you can apply to, or when you have already applied.

Your applications come first, under **My applications**; then the calls you can apply to now, under **Open calls**. The calls not open yet or already closed are folded under **Other calls**. Each call shows its dates and a link to **Read the call**. Read it before applying: it explains the requirements, the documents and how the places are awarded.

The Erasmus team may exclude, giving the reason, students with disciplinary records or serious coexistence incidents this course, having heard the teaching team and the coexistence team (article 6 of the call). Each conduct warning also takes points off your score.

## 2. Fill in the application

Click **Apply** and fill in the form:

1. **Documents**, first. Each one says what it must contain. The ones marked **Required** are mandatory:
    - **Europass CV in English**, made with the Europass editor (the link is in the form), with a photo: what you study, what you are learning in the cycle, your strong points and a short "About me". It is the CV we will send to the company of your internship, so keep it brief and clear. **PDF only.**
    - **Presentation video in English** (about a minute): save it in Google Drive with your school account, click **Share** and, under **General access**, choose **Institut Puig Castellar** as **Viewer**. Copy the link and paste it in the form.
    - Your official language certificates, if you have any. If you are a minor, no authorisation is needed to apply: your family will sign the commitment letter later, if you get a place.
2. **Fewer opportunities**: the form sums up which situations count (annex 2 of the call lists the documents that prove each one). If you can prove one, tick the box: the field to upload the evidence appears right below it, and it becomes required. Only the Erasmus team sees it.
3. **Anything else** you want the Erasmus team to know, at the end.

The use of your image in the project's dissemination follows your image rights authorisation of this course: the form shows it, and you are not asked again.

Click **Save draft** to continue later.

## 3. Submit it

Click **Submit application**. You can only submit it when every required document is attached. You will receive a receipt by email with the files received (for a link, the link itself, so you can check it). If the student is a minor, the family receives a copy.

If you upload a document again, the newest file is the one that counts: the earlier ones are kept and marked *Earlier version*, and the receipt lists only the newest (a document with several files, such as the language certificates, keeps them all). Each file shows the day it was uploaded. While the application is a draft, **Remove** takes away a file uploaded by mistake; once it is submitted, ask the Erasmus team. If a document has a model, **Download the model** is next to it.

Until the closing date you can still change it: the form shows when you submitted it, and the button becomes **Send the changes**. Each time you send it you will receive a new receipt. After the closing date the application can no longer be changed.

## 4. If you are asked for corrections

If a document is missing or needs changes, you will receive an email with the list and the deadline. Open your application on the portal: the box at the top says so, with the deadline, and the documents to correct are marked in red. Attach the new files and click **Send the corrections**. If you don't correct it in time, the application will be excluded.

## 5. The interview

If your application is admitted, you will receive an email with the date and place of your personal interview, at least 24 hours ahead. You will also see them on your application in the portal, in the box at the top. Part of the interview is in English. Arrive 15 minutes early: not attending excludes you, unless for a justified reason of force majeure. If you are excluded, you receive an email with the reason, which the portal also shows.

## 6. Your results

When the Erasmus+ Commission publishes a list (the admission list, the provisional resolution, the definitive resolution), you receive an email with your result and you see it on your application in the portal, under **Results**: your position in your modality, your total score and the points of each criterion. The newest result is open; click the title of an earlier one to see it.

Click **See the published list** to see the whole list. It does not show names: each person appears only with four digits of their identity document, for example ***4567**. Your own row is highlighted and marked **You**. Applicants with the same score are ordered by the tie-break criteria of the call and, if still tied, by a draw. You can also download it as a PDF.

If you are on the **reserve list**, your result says which reserve number you are in your modality: places left free are offered to the reserves in that order. If you did not reach the minimum score of the call, the result says so, with the minimum.

While the provisional resolution is out, the badge at the top of your application shows your provisional result (for example *Reserve (provisional)*).

**Claims.** If you disagree with the provisional result, open **I want to claim** under it, write what you claim and why, attach any files that support it and click **Send the claim**, before the claims deadline. EMS records when it arrived. You receive an email confirming that it arrived, and another one with the Erasmus team's response, which also appears under **My claims**.

**Appeal.** Against the definitive resolution you can lodge an appeal (*recurs d'alçada*) before the Head of Studies within one month from the day after its publication: write to the Erasmus team, who records it. The Head of Studies answers it, and you receive the answer by email.

## 7. Accept or renounce your place

If you are **selected** in the definitive resolution, your application shows the place offered and the deadline to answer, with the period of your modality, its destinations with their places and your position in the modality: you choose a destination later, by turns in order of position, so you know what you may get before accepting. The email telling you that you have a place lists them too:

- Click **I accept the place** to accept it.
- If you cannot go, open **I want to renounce the place**, write the reason if you want, and click **Confirm that I renounce the place**.

You receive an email confirming your answer; if you are a minor, your family receives a copy (also of the place offer). Without an answer by the deadline, the place is considered renounced and goes to the next person on the reserve list: you receive an email saying so. If you are on the **reserve list** and a place becomes free, you will receive an email and the offer will appear on your application, with its own deadline.

## 8. Prepare your mobility

When you accept the place, your application shows **Your mobility**.

**Choose your destination.** If your modality has several destinations, the selected choose in order of the list, starting with the first one. When it is your turn you receive an email: open your application, pick one of the destinations with free places for your cycle and click **Choose this destination** before the deadline shown. If you don't answer in time, you don't lose the place, but you lose your turn: you will choose among the destinations left once everyone else has chosen. If none of them suits you, or you are not available for their dates, talk to the Erasmus team (erasmus@elpuig.xeill.net) to find an alternative: a destination you find yourself, or one through an organisation the centre works with. If your modality has only one destination, you are assigned to it directly.

**Your own destination.** You can also find a company or entity in another Erasmus+ country yourself. Open **Propose my own destination**, fill in the entity, its contact person, the country, the city and the dates, attach its **written commitment** to take you, and click **Send the proposal**. The entity must also write to erasmus@elpuig.xeill.net. The Erasmus team validates it; if not, you will receive the reason and you can choose among the centre's destinations.

**Documents and steps.** Once you have a destination, the card is split into tabs and opens on the one with something to do now: **Summary** (the place, the host entity, the dates and your grant), **My record** (the commitment letter and the documents, with a number saying what you still have to do now), **Steps**, and later **During the stay** and **On return**. You find there:

- The **documents** to hand in before leaving and on return, who must **sign** each one and its deadline. Your identity document, bank account and personal details are already in EMS. Upload the European Health Insurance Card (both sides) as soon as you have it, and, if you are a minor, the authorisation to travel abroad processed with the Mossos d'Esquadra: within a month of getting your destination, and the OLS certificate with your English level in the same time. If it applies to you, the inclusion documentation (fewer opportunities, grants, special needs): only the Erasmus team sees it. In a higher-education (GS) stay of 30 days or fewer, also the **virtual component**: a PDF with your preparation, which you can upload from a month before leaving until the day before (information about the city, how you will get to the company and how much transport costs, and the exercises on the technologies and tasks of the host company). The grant agreement and the learning agreement are uploaded filled in by the Erasmus team: download them, sign them and upload them signed (ideally three months before leaving, a month before at the latest). The Erasmus team also uploads your insurance policy, for you to download, and the certificate of stay: download it, have your tutor at the host company sign it at the end of the stay and upload it signed within a month of your return. While anything is missing you will receive a reminder every week. Sign the documents outside EMS, attach the signed copy and click **Upload the documents**. If the Erasmus team asks for a change, you will see its comment in red. Each document shows its status: only what you have to do now is red (*To hand in*, *Needs changes*); a document the Erasmus team has to upload first says *The Erasmus team uploads it*, and one that is not due yet says *Not yet*. The documents for after the stay are folded until then. Documents that don't apply to you (those for minors, if you are an adult) are not shown. Once you have accepted the place, your results are folded too. Next to who signs each document you see whether the Erasmus team has already checked each signature (green tick) or not yet.
- The **commitment letter** (annex 3 of the call): read it on the portal, tick that you accept it and click **Accept and sign**. EMS keeps it signed. If you are a minor, your family signs it too: it receives an email and signs it from its own access to the portal, in **Erasmus+**, where it reads its own sentence ("As the family, we know the commitments that ... takes on in this stay and we accept them") above your letter. From the moment you apply, your family already sees your application there, read only: its state, each document and, for those with a model, **Download the model**.
- The **steps** to follow (preparation sessions, the online language course...). The Erasmus team ticks them when they are done.

**During the stay.** Every week, answer the **weekly self-assessment** on the card: what you did, what you learned, how you rate your week from 1 to 5 and what was hardest for you. Each week can be answered from its first day, and only once. If you forget one, you will receive a reminder. The Erasmus team reads it and it is kept in your record.

**On return: EU Survey.** When the stay is over, the card asks you to answer the **EU Survey**, the final report of the European Commission (you receive its link by email from the European Commission), and shows its deadline. Once you have answered it, click **I have answered it**; the Erasmus team checks it.

If you have to renounce, open **I want to renounce the place**. Renouncing without a justified cause may mean paying the expenses already committed (tickets, bookings).

If you graduate this course and do the mobility in the summer, you keep access to the portal until it ends.

## 9. Follow your application

At the top of your application, a bar with the steps of the call shows where it is: application, document check, interview, resolution, place, preparation, stay and return. The steps done are ticked, the current one is highlighted and, if the application ended in a step (for example, excluded), that step shows a cross. Below it, a box says **what to do now** and by when (in yellow), or what you are waiting for. Once your application can no longer change, the call's text and the application as you sent it are folded under **The call and my application**, at the end.

The Erasmus+ page shows the state of your application, in colour: *Draft*, *Submitted*, *Corrections requested*, *Admitted*, *Excluded*, *Selected*, *Reserve*, *Not selected*, *Place accepted* or *Renounced*, and the state of your mobility: *Defining the mobility*, *Preparing*, *Closed* or *Cancelled*.

For any question, write to the Erasmus coordination: **erasmus@elpuig.xeill.net**.
